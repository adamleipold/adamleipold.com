# AI Is Not a Surprise to the Architect

> *"For in him all things were created… and in him all things hold together."* — Colossians 1:16–17

Every few weeks a new headline insists we should be afraid. *The machines are coming.* Something ancient in us tightens — the same instinct that has met every new and powerful thing since the first spark was struck from the first stone.

But fear was never the right first response to power. Discernment was.

Consider two building projects in the book of Genesis. Both used the best technology of their day. One was a tower, raised on the plains of Shinar by people who said, *"Let us make a name for ourselves."* The other was a boat, built by a man who simply obeyed. Same tools. Same human ingenuity. One ended in confusion and scattering; the other carried life through the flood. The difference was never the technology — it was the heart with its hand on the tool.

We forget how far back this goes. The first person Scripture ever calls *"filled with the Spirit of God"* was not a prophet or a king. It was a craftsman named Bezalel — an artist and an engineer, gifted *"with skill, ability and knowledge in all kinds of crafts"* to build a place where heaven and earth would meet (Exodus 31). God's first Spirit-filled worker built things with his hands. Technology, in the right hands, has always been holy ground.

So here is what I keep coming back to: artificial intelligence is not a rupture in the story. It is *downstream* of it. The One who flung the stars into space also authored the universe whose deep properties make all of this possible — the physics of light, the strange order of mathematics, the fact that sand can be taught to reason. *"In him all things hold together"* includes the very laws that make a thinking machine. The Architect who set them is not caught off guard when His creatures finally discover what they permit.

We have been here before. Five centuries ago a new machine terrified the powerful — the printing press. It flooded the world with words, good and evil alike: propaganda and lies, yes, but also, for the first time, the Scriptures in the language of the plowboy. God took the last great information revolution and used it to put His Word into ordinary hands. He was not afraid of it. He redeemed it.

I've watched this up close lately. The very same tools that could be pointed at deception, at manipulation, at burying people in noise, can instead be turned to gather what is true and carry it to someone who is searching. Fire cooks the meal or burns the house. The press prints the Scripture or the slander. The intelligence illuminates or it deceives. The tool amplifies whatever heart is aiming it — which means the real question is never *what can it do,* but *what is it pointed at, and by whom.*

If you are uneasy about all of this, you're in good company; so was every generation that met a power it did not yet understand. But you can set the fear down. Nothing being built right now is a surprise to the God who was holding the whole thing together before the first circuit was ever drawn. He is not behind. He is not threatened. He is, as ever, at work — ready to take what human hands have made and bend it toward the oldest purpose there is: bringing people home to Jesus, who was there in the beginning, and is holding it all together still.

The machines are not coming for that. If anything — they may yet carry someone to it.
