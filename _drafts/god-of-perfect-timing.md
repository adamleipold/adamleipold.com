# The God of Perfect Timing

> *"He has made everything beautiful in its time."* — Ecclesiastes 3:11

There is a particular kind of ache that doesn't come from things going wrong. It comes from things not yet going *right.* The prayer you've prayed for years. The door that hasn't opened. The season that has run so long you've started to wonder if God lost the address to your life.

If that's where you are, I want to tell you the thing the whole of Scripture keeps insisting on: **the delay is not the denial.**

Look at how God works with the people He loves most. He gives David a crown as a boy — and then sends him back to the sheep, then to a cave, then to years of running for his life, long before he ever sits on a throne. He gives Joseph a dream of his family bowing before him — and the next stop is a pit, then slavery, then a prison for a crime he didn't commit. Every one of them received the promise, and then received a long, dark stretch of waiting that looked nothing like the promise.

And then — the word Scripture loves — *suddenly.* Suddenly Joseph is standing before Pharaoh. Suddenly the shepherd is a king. The waiting was never wasted; it was the very thing that shaped them into people who could carry what was coming.

Here is the hard, freeing truth underneath it: *"My thoughts are not your thoughts, neither are your ways my ways," says the Lord* (Isaiah 55). We want the straight line. He is writing a better story than the straight line would allow. What feels like God being slow is very often God being thorough — preparing you, and preparing the thing, so the two arrive ready for each other.

And you can trust that, because of *who* is doing the timing. This is not a distant clockmaker. This is the God who flung the stars into space and yet numbers the hairs on your head — big enough to be in no hurry, near enough to know exactly where you are. The same One who, *"when the fullness of time had come,"* sent His own Son — not early, not late, but at the exact appointed hour a waiting world had ached for (Galatians 4:4). If His timing was perfect for the salvation of the whole world, it is not going to fail for you.

So in the meantime — and the meantime is where most of life is actually lived — don't measure God's faithfulness by the speed of your answer. Keep believing. Keep praising in the waiting room, the way Paul and Silas sang at midnight before a single chain fell off. Your *suddenly* is on its way, and it will be worth the wait — because *He has made everything beautiful in its time.*

Not your time. His. And His is always, in the end, perfect.
